03 / ALIGNMENT
Trust needs several kinds of evidence
1
Values and training
State the desired behavior.
Reward honesty and useful uncertainty.
2
Tests that can surprise it
Use unfamiliar cases and adversarial tests.
Check whether the result helps people.
3
Limits on action
Match access to the task.
Require review for consequential actions.
4
Accountability after release
Record failures, invite complaints,
and make correction possible.
Practical discussion proposals, not a proven recipe for aligning superintelligence. Better training alone is not a safety guarantee.

EVIDENCE NOTES
Three headlines, read carefully
The flying Roadster
The episode describes a demo and gives performance and
street-legality claims. This deck uses it as a discussion prompt, without
certifying those details.
The 84% robotics drop
The transcript quotes $2.36 to $0.38 from July to September. That pair
was not verified in the primary page checked. Slide 6 uses its published
$1.14 vs $0.90 comparison instead.
The physics score
The primary preprint reports 98%, or 49 out of 50. It does not establish
a perfect score, autonomous discovery, or a date when physics will be
solved.

EVIDENCE NOTES
How to read this presentation
Reported results
Benchmark numbers and historical prize results link to the
relevant primary source. A benchmark is evidence within its
test conditions.
Ideas from the conversation
New prize concepts, abundant essentials and future human
purpose appear as proposals or discussion themes. They are
not forecasts presented as facts.
Illustrations and exercises
Artwork is conceptual. The cafe example, reliability
calculation, alignment checklist and audience challenges are
explanatory additions.
